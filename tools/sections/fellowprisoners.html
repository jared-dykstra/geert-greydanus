The men Geert named: fellow prisoners in Vught, 1944

On his Red Cross form, signed in Rotterdam on 12 August 1945, Geert was asked which fellow prisoners who had died in captivity he could name. He named fourteen men who had died, and two cellmates who had been moved to other camps, nearly all from his months in Kamp Vught (Greydanus 1b; see the form in the timeline). On a second form he added one more, a man who died on the death march. He visited the families of Rohaan, Heinen, Van Braak and Berlijn himself, and met Stigter's family.

Almost all of them can now be identified. Most were arrested in April and May 1944, many in the same 20 April sweep as Geert, and held in Vught while he was in its bunker. Almost all were then executed: in the dunes at Overveen on 6 June 1944, at Fort Rhijnauwen near Utrecht on 25 July 1944, or in Vught itself in August 1944. The link between Geert and each man rests on Geert's own list; no other source yet names him beside any of them. Where an identification rests on a match between his description and the records, it says "probably".

Two sources from the time confirm much of the list. On 7 June 1944 the German police chief for the Netherlands announced that the Polizeistandgericht in 's-Hertogenbosch had sentenced fifteen men to death. They include Heinen, Poulie, Kruizinga and Rohaan, "den opperwachtmeester der marechaussee Teunis Wortman te Neede", Blaauwgeers, the painter Carel Verschuur of Wageningen, and "den hotelhouder Jacobus Westland" and "den bioscoopeigenaar Willem Westland te Wageningen" (German death-sentence notice, 7 Jun 1944). The list also names "den landbouwer Hendrik Wiggers te Dale", born 29 December 1886: Hendrik Jan Wiggers of Aalten, a different man from the H. Wiggers who hid Geert near Winterswijk in April 1943 (see above). His sentence was not carried out; he died at Nordhausen in February 1945. And in May 1945 a Catholic prisoner who had been held in cell 154 of the Vught bunker described the evening of 5 June 1944, when he spoke through the spy-hole of his door to the condemned men in the cells around him: "Kees Poulie ...; Dirk Wortman uit Neede, die bij een distributie-overval geholpen had; Karel Verschuur uit Wageningen; Rinus Veth uit Papendrecht en Kees de Jong, Old Bekkink, Marcusse Hendriks, Beerman en zooveel anderen uit Nijmegen". The bunker's guard, he writes, was SS-Unterscharführer Popey, "den bunkerbeul", the same man Hoekstra names among Geert's torturers ("Feest der vervulling", 19 May 1945). The writer is not named and does not mention Geert, but his account puts several of the men on Geert's list in the bunker cells in the days before 6 June.

Willem Arnold Rohaan (Neede, 1912 – Overveen, 1944)

Born in Neede on 26 October 1912, he worked his parents' farm and was deputy commander of the local Ordedienst (OD). He hid Jewish and other onderduikers on the farm, carried ration cards, and gathered information for the Aalten knokploeg. After the betrayal by Willy Markus, his friend Wortman and fellow villager Kruizinga were arrested in April and May 1944; Rohaan refused to go into hiding for his parents' sake and was arrested at home early on 5 May 1944. He went via Arnhem to Kamp Vught, was sentenced to death on 2 June and shot at Overveen on 6 June 1944 (Eerebegraafplaats Bloemendaal: Willem Arnold Rohaan). Geert gave his age as about 30; he was 31.

Hendrik Pieter (Henk) Heinen (Eibergen, 1914 – Overveen, 1944)

Geert spelled him "Heynen". Born in Eibergen on 28 November 1914, he was a typographer, bookseller and photographer who ran his father's printing shop and printed the underground paper Trouw. Through Markus's betrayal he was arrested by the Sicherheitspolizei on the evening of 20 April 1944, the day Geert was taken, and went via Arnhem to Kamp Vught. He was shot at Overveen on 6 June 1944, aged 29 (Eerebegraafplaats Bloemendaal: Hendrik Pieter Heinen). The German notice charged that he had handed the gun of "den wachtmeester Poulie", after Poulie fled, to the leader of a knokploeg (German death-sentence notice, 7 Jun 1944). He is very likely the husband of the "Mevrouw Heinen" to whom J. v.d. Hauw wrote about the Vught bunker (see 21 Apr – 9 Aug 1944).

Martinus Antonius (Martin) Lelivelt (Lichtenvoorde, 1896 – Fort Rhijnauwen, 1944)

Geert's "Lebbeveld [?] ±55 j. Lichtenvoorde" is overwritten and hard to read, but it ends in "-veld", and of the Lichtenvoorde men who died after the 20 April 1944 raid (H. Blaauwgeers, W. Doppen, E. Heusinkveld, A. and M. Kettering, M. Lelivelt, A. Slot) only Lelivelt fits the name (Nationaal Onderduikmuseum). Born in Lichtenvoorde on 17 January 1896, a carpenter who ran a building firm with his brothers, he hid Jews, onderduikers, resistance men and airmen for years; a 1945 obituary asks how many "Engelse en Amerikaanse piloten, Fransen en Yoego-Slaviërs" had found shelter in his house ("In memoriam", 26 May 1945). He was arrested on 20 April 1944, tortured in Kamp Vught without giving anything away, moved to the Wolvenplein prison in Utrecht and shot at Fort Rhijnauwen on 25 July 1944, aged 48 (Oorlogsgravenstichting: Martinus Antonius Lelivelt).

Geert's age of about 55 fits another man from the same raid better: Johannes Wilhelmus (Willem) Doppen, born in Lichtenvoorde on 24 September 1884, a postman and later grocer, arrested at home on 20 April 1944 (a Lichtenvoorde report of June 1945 gives 24 April: Lichtenvoorde news, 12 Jun 1945), sent via Kamp Vught and Oranienburg to Buchenwald, where he died on 8 March 1945 (Oorlogsgravenstichting: Johannes Wilhelmus Doppen). The name on the form points to Lelivelt; the age points to Doppen.

Teunis Klaas Wortman (Geldermalsen, 1911 – Overveen, 1944)

Geert could not remember the name of "a marechaussee from Neede", and "Blauwgeers" was written in above the line. Jo Blaauwgeers was a marechaussee, but he was posted at Harreveld in the municipality of Lichtenvoorde, not at Neede (Wikipedia NL: Jo Blaauwgeers). The Neede marechaussee is Teunis Klaas Wortman, born in Geldermalsen on 27 November 1911, opperwachtmeester der marechaussee, shot at Overveen on 6 June 1944 (Erelijst: Teunis Klaas Wortman). His death was registered in Neede, which points to Neede as his last home (Neede death register, 6 Jul 1944), and Rohaan's biography calls him Rohaan's friend. The German death-sentence notice gives him as "Teunis Wortman te Neede" (German death-sentence notice, 7 Jun 1944); the Vught bunker account of May 1945 calls him "Dirk Wortman uit Neede". His widow thanked Neede for its sympathy on 18 July 1944, "mede namens ... dochtertje" (notice of thanks, Neede, 18 Jul 1944). Both Wortman and Blaauwgeers were shot on 6 June 1944.

Cornelis Coenraad (Kees) Poulie (Den Helder, 1919 – Overveen, 1944)

Geert's "Marechaussee Paul[?] ±22 j. Varsseveld" is Kees Poulie, the Varsseveld marechaussee who brought the airman Babcock to the Satter farm (see 4–6 Jan 1944). Born in Den Helder on 5 July 1919, wachtmeester der marechaussee (Erelijst: Cornelis Coenraad Poulie), he was shot on 6 June 1944, aged 24; his death was registered in Wisch, the municipality of Varsseveld (Wisch death register, 7 Jul 1944).

Albertus Hendrikus (Albert) Marcusse (Batenburg, 1903 – Overveen, 1944) and the Nijmegen police group

Born in Batenburg on 2 November 1903, Marcusse was hoofdrechercheur (chief detective) of the Nijmegen police (Oorlogsgravenstichting: Albertus Hendrikus Marcusse) and a member of the resistance group of Nijmegen policemen around Wim Beerman (Wikipedia NL: Albert Marcusse). The group's chief suspects, Herman Oolbekkink, Albertus H. Marcusse, Wilhelmus A. (Wim) Beerman, Bartholomeus Th. (Bart) Hendriks and the civilian Cees de Jong, were sentenced to death on 19 May 1944 and shot in the dunes near Overveen on 6 June 1944 (Schunck, Verzetsmonument Nijmegen; Oorlogsdoden Nijmegen: A.H. Marcusse). Geert's second Nijmegen policeman is one of Beerman, Hendriks and Oolbekkink. The Vught bunker account of May 1945 places all five in the bunker cells, condemned, on the night of 5 June 1944: "Kees de Jong, Old Bekkink, Marcusse Hendriks, Beerman en zooveel anderen uit Nijmegen" (see above).

Gerrit Jan van den Boogerd (Zwartewaal, 1908 – Fort Rhijnauwen, 1944)

Geert's "Gerrit v.d. Bogaard ±30 j. candidaat-notaris" is "Oom Gert", the leader of the military resistance group whom Markus deceived and who was arrested in Zutphen on the morning of 20 April 1944 (see 20 April 1944). Born in Zwartewaal on 22 August 1908, candidaat-notaris (Erelijst: Gerrit Jan van den Boogerd), he was shot at Fort Rhijnauwen on 25 July 1944 (Oorlogsgravenstichting: Gerrit Jan van den Boogerd), the same day as Lelivelt.

Willem Westland (Wageningen, 1900–1944) and Jacobus Westland (Wageningen, 1902–1944)

Geert remembered only that they were brothers who owned a hotel and a cinema, "in Wageningen?", and that one had his birthday on 1 June. Jacobus Westland was born in Wageningen on 1 June 1902; with his elder brother Willem, born there on 8 April 1900, he owned the Oranje hotel and the Luxor cinema on the Hoogstraat. They went into hiding with Hendrik Veldhuis in Slochteren, where a raid at 4 a.m. on 23 May 1944 caught them with Klaas Woltjer. They were taken to the Scholtenhuis in Groningen and on to Kamp Vught, sentenced to death by the Polizeistandgericht in 's-Hertogenbosch with Carel Verschuur, and shot in the dunes at Bloemendaal on 6 June 1944 (Wageningen 1940–1945: Jacobus Westland; Willem Westland). Jacobus was reburied in the Jewish cemetery in Wageningen in 1945.

Johannes (Jo) Berlijn (Rotterdam, 1906 – Vught, 1944)

Geert's "J. Berlijn ±42 j. koopman Rotterdam". Born in Rotterdam on 16 February 1906, a trader in chemicals, Jo Berlijn worked in the forgery centre of the Rotterdam LO under the cover name "Van Keulen". He fell into German hands in April 1944 and was killed in Kamp Vught on 30 August 1944, aged 38 (Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught: Berlijn, Johannes). Geert's first hiding place, on 9–12 April 1943, was his house. On his 1946 form Geert wrote that he stayed "aan de Sichemstraat 14 p/a of ten huize van J. Berlijn" (rehabilitation form, 1946; see 9–12 Apr 1943), and Jo Berlijn's widow, announcing that he had been shot in Vught, gave her "Oud adres Sichemstraat 14". The same page carries his parents' notice, which confirms his cover name, "Schuilnaam van Keulen" (family notices, June 1945). The notices give his age as 36 and 33; the museum's birth date makes him 38. Geert visited the Berlijn family after the war.

Johan Gerard (Joop) Westerweel (Zutphen, 1899 – Vught, 1944)

Geert's "J. Westerweel ±45 j. onderw. Montessorischool" is Joop Westerweel, head of the Montessori school in Rotterdam, who helped Jews escape to Spain. He wrote letters in the bunker prison at Vught and was shot there on 11 August 1944, two days after Geert left the camp (Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught: Westerweel, Johan Gerard; Wikipedia NL). His birth date differs between sources: the Oorlogsgravenstichting gives 25 January 1899 in Zutphen and notes: "Hielp joden naar Spanje vluchten" (Oorlogsgravenstichting: Johan Gerard Westerweel), the Vught museum 25 February 1899.

"Karel" and Karel Roelof Verschuur (Wageningen, 1919 – Overveen, 1944)

Geert remembered only "a certain 'Karel' from the cell next to 93", and listed him among the prisoners moved elsewhere, not among the dead. One Karel is known to have been in the Vught bunker that summer: "Karel Verschuur uit Wageningen", among the condemned men in the bunker cells on 5 June 1944 (see above). Karel Roelof Verschuur, a painter born in Wageningen on 15 April 1919 and living in Olst, was arrested at 's-Hertogenbosch station on 22 April 1944 and shot in the dunes at Overveen on 6 June 1944 (Oorlogsbronnen: Karel Roelof Verschuur); the German notice gives him as "Carel Verschuur", born 18 April 1919. He is unlikely to be Geert's Karel: he was dead a month before Geert moved to cell 93 around 8 July, and Geert did not count his Karel among the dead. Geert's Karel is not yet identified.

Eemke van der Veen (Nieuweschans, 1902 – Vught, 1944)

Geert's cellmate of about three and a half weeks was a "Secr. Ambt.", a clerk at the town hall, from Haren in Groningen. Possibly this was Eemke van der Veen, born in Nieuweschans on 5 June 1902, who lived at Meerweg 54 in Haren and was arrested at the Haren town hall on 2 May 1944, accused of supplying identity papers. He was shot in Kamp Vught on 22 August 1944 (Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught: Veen, Eemke van der; Groningen 40-45: Harense ambtenaren in verzet). Geert, though, listed his cellmate among the men transported to other camps, not among the dead, so the match is uncertain.

Aaron Akker (Sneek, 1895 – 1945)

On his Stichting 1940–'45 form Geert wrote that of the four Dutch Jews with him at Niederorschel, "de ±50 jarige jood Akker uit Leeuwarden" died on the march to Buchenwald (Greydanus 2b). This is probably Aaron Akker, born in Sneek on 24 November 1895, who worked at the Frico dairy in Leeuwarden and lived at Slotmakersstraat 6. Joods Monument records his death in Buchenwald on 6 April 1945, during the days of the march; his wife Dora Akker-Herz was murdered in Auschwitz in 1944 (Joods Monument: Aaron Akker).
