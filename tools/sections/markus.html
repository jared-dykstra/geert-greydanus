The betrayal: Willy Markus

The raid on the Satter farm on 20 April 1944 was one of many that day. All of them came from the work of one man, Willy Markus, an informer for the German security police (the SD) who had spent three months inside the Achterhoek resistance. The Germans had a name for the Satter house: "het Broeinest", the hotbed (Oorlogsgravenstichting, Hendrik Satter).

Who Willy Markus was

Willem (Willy) Markus was born in Ambt Doetinchem on 24 March 1902. When the war began he ran the café his late father had built up at Hommelstraat 22 in Arnhem. It became a meeting place for black marketeers and German soldiers, and Markus bought drink for the German army and resold it at a profit. In 1943 he was jailed for receiving stolen goods, and came out as a V-Mann, a paid informer, for the SD. He learned the work from another Arnhem man, Johnny de Droog. (Wikipedia NL; Oorlogsbronnen)

How he worked, January – April 1944

  • Arnhem jail. His first job was to befriend a warder who smuggled notes out of the Arnhem prison for resistance prisoners. Markus and De Droog read the notes before they were delivered, and in January 1944 fourteen people were arrested in Velp and Rheden.

  • "Willy van Erp". In January 1944, under that name, he made contact with Gerrit van den Boogerd ("Oom Gert"), who led a military resistance group of more than 500 men. He passed himself off as a secret agent of the Dutch government in London. He won trust by bringing weapons, among them a carbine he put in the hands of Cornelis Ruizendaal ("Zwarte Kees"), leader of the Aalten knokploeg (KP), the armed squad. Because the Germans controlled some of London's radio links, he could even have agreed phrases broadcast on Radio Oranje. (Het Grote Gebod, pp. 482–484; Wikipedia NL)

  • Kees Poulie of Varsseveld. Poulie, a sergeant of the Varsseveld marechaussee (military police) who had gone into hiding with an American pilot, asked Markus for a way to England. Markus offered to take him and his fiancée, Annie Droog. They were arrested at Oosterbeek station in early March 1944. Poulie was later executed in Vught, and what the SD got from his interrogation went onto its list of names. (Wikipedia NL; Het Grote Gebod, pp. 480, 483)

20 April 1944

The SD struck on 20 April, a day before Zwarte Kees planned to attack the home-guard post in Ruurlo where Markus lodged. In the morning Markus picked up Van den Boogerd and his deputy Dick Vermeij, who were arrested first. He then drove a borrowed lorry to Beltrum to collect four KP men for a "weapons delivery". They grew suspicious when they saw blood on the floor of the lorry and got out at Doesburg. Two of them were caught at Ellecom; Zwarte Kees was killed in a gunfight in Doesburg, and the chemist who had sheltered them, Philip Gastelaars, was shot at the Avegoor SS school. In the afternoon the raids followed in Aalten, Eibergen, Lichtenvoorde, Lintvelde, Neede and Varsseveld, where Geert, Hendrik Satter and Anne Schaap were taken. (Wikipedia NL; Het Grote Gebod, p. 484)

The next day 27 prisoners were sent from Arnhem to Vught. Four of them jumped from the lorry on the way, as the timeline's 20–21 April entry also records. About half of the rest died before a firing squad or in the camps; most of the KP men were shot in the dunes at Overveen on 6 June 1944. Two days after the raids Markus also lured the painter Karel Verschuur, linked to the Aalten KP, into a trap at 's-Hertogenbosch station. A US Army helper file from Lichtenvoorde names him too: "By treachery of Willy Markens several workers have been shot" (Leemreize helper file).

After the war

Once the Achterhoek knew who had betrayed it, Markus was of no more use there. After a failed attempt in Roermond to trap a resistance leader who in turn hoped to kidnap him, he asked to be sent to work in Germany. After the war he feigned insanity and spent a long time in psychiatric institutions. On 5 July 1949 the Bijzonder Gerechtshof (special court) in Arnhem sentenced him to life imprisonment. In December 1952 the sentence was reduced to 18 years, and he was released in November 1956. He died in Enschede on 24 March 1978. (Wikipedia NL; Oorlogsbronnen)

What this adds for Geert

  • A sharper lead for his trial file. Markus's file in the CABR (the archive of post-war collaboration cases at the Nationaal Archief) belongs to the Arnhem special court, verdict of 5 July 1949. Such files often list the people a traitor gave up, and may show whether the SD came to the Satter farm for Geert, for the airmen, or for Hendrik Satter's Trouw work.

  • Two books cited by Wikipedia may name the Varsseveld arrests: Henny Bennink, Bezetting en verzet: de gemeente Lichtenvoorde en de omliggende gemeenten in de Tweede Wereldoorlog (2005), and Erik Schaap, De levens van Johnny de Droog (2020).

  • Not the same Wiggers, probably. Het Grote Gebod lists "Hendrik Wiggers (Oom Henk)" among Markus's victims, arrested on 20 April 1944. Geert's second hiding place, in April 1943, was "bij H. Wiggers, hoeve Plak-an te Winterswijk". The Wiggers who was arrested is Hendrik Jan Wiggers of Vellegendijk 17, Aalten, a farmer who hid a Jewish family and was later recognised by Yad Vashem; he died at Mittelbau-Dora in February 1945 (Synagoge Aalten; Oorlogsbronnen). His farm was in Aalten, not Winterswijk, so he is probably not Geert's host. Unconfirmed either way.

Sources disagree on one detail: Wikipedia has Gastelaars shot the day after the raid, Het Grote Gebod the same evening.
