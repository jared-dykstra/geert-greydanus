How his account reached the Satter farm

  1. Berlin, February 1943. Ubbink visited Gerstein in Berlin and found him in despair. Ubbink later wrote: "He asked me if I could get in touch with people who had radio contact with London. I told him I could. He asked me, no — more than that, he begged me to transmit this story to England, so it would be known all over the world, and that the German people could be warned." (Brayard, p. 161)

  2. Doesburg. Ubbink's foundry was a distribution point for the underground papers Vrij Nederland and Trouw. He passed Gerstein's account to a resistance man he knew only as "Cor": Cornelis van der Hooft from Overschie, Rotterdam, the chief distributor of the illegal papers in the Achterhoek and one of the early Trouw men. Twenty years later Ubbink recognised him from a photograph: "We called him Cor." (Van Kaam 1995; De Gelderlander, 5 May 2012, reproduced in Hoekstra 2013, pp. 22–23)

  3. The Satter farmhouse, 25 March 1943. Van der Hooft came to the farm in Sinderen and wrote out a four-page Dutch version, headed Tötungsanstalten in Polen ("Killing installations in Poland"), with Hendrik Satter and his son Jo watching. Jo remembered: "I can still see Cor sitting there writing. He was actually completely shaken by it. ... When Cor was finished, he said: 'This is such a terrible thing; you must read this.'" (Van Kaam 1995; Brayard places the meeting "in the outskirts of Doesburg", p. 163)

  4. The chicken coop. Jo's sister Willemien, then 21, overheard them: "They did not know what to do with it. I heard them say 'unbelievable' a few times. They thought it might be a trap. Could the writer be trusted, and if so, were he and his family safe, because he could easily be recognised from the report? In the end I heard Jo say: 'put it away in the chicken coop.'" (Trouw, 30 Jan 2014, translated) The plan was to get the officer and his family into hiding first, with the Satters finding an address, and only then publish. Van der Hooft went to take that message back to Ubbink, but never came back for the document. (Hoekstra 2013, p. 23)

  5. London, April–September 1943. The account did reach the Dutch government in exile. A typed version almost identical to the farm's, marked "Rappt. Nr. 61", probably arrived in London on 24 April 1943 or a few days before, "perhaps by radio". The minister of war, Van Lidth de Jeude, paraphrased it in his diary on 8 May. In the summer of 1943 Van Haersma, of the government in exile, decided to ask the Inter-Allied Information Committee in New York to publish it; Brayard finds it "difficult to determine" whether it was ever passed on, and his account ends "in uncertainty". As far as is known it was never published. The London copy was only found in 1992. (Brayard, pp. 165–167)

12 Apr – late Apr 1943Hides "bij H. Wiggers, hoeve Plak-an te Winterswijk". This is Hans Wiggers (died 2005, aged 94), whose small farm Plakan stood where the Jasmijnlaan in Winterswijk is now (OudWinterswijk: Hans Wiggers). Plakan sheltered people in hiding until the end of the war; Hans Wiggers: "We had Jews from Winterswijk whom we already knew from before the war, and later also those we didn't know" (Achterhoek Nieuws, 4 May 2020). Yad Vashem recognised him as Righteous Among the Nations in 1979 for sheltering Aaron van Gelder in the round-up of 8 Oct 1941 (Yad Vashem: Hans Wiggers). Not to be confused with Hendrik Jan Wiggers of the "Bosberg" farm in Aalten, who hid the Jewish Meijler family and was arrested on 20 Apr 1944 (Yad Vashem: Hendrik and Aleida Wiggers; Oorlogsbronnen: Hendrik Jan Wiggers)Geert form p. 1; Verzetsrapport; J. Satter letter 1952
Late Apr 1943 – 20 Apr 1944Hides with the H. Satter family between Sinderen and Varsseveld, "onderbroken door eenige weken, bij familie en kennissen in Friesland" (interrupted by some weeks with relatives and friends in Friesland). The farm was a resistance centre: the Stichting report counts Satter among "de medeoprichters van het illegale blad 'Trouw'" (Verzetsrapport), and the farm sometimes sheltered more than 30 people in hiding. A 2014 Trouw article by Meindert van der Kaaij, based on Willemien (Wil) Satter's memories, says the first LO meeting was held on the farm and that Trouw's leadership met there regularly after the paper was founded in Aerdenhout on 30 Jan 1943 (Trouw, text read from Jared's blog copy). Hoekstra adds that neither Wil nor Geert was allowed into those meetings: "De dochter van Hendrik Satter, Wil, mocht niet aanwezig zijn bij deze vergaderingen, evenmin als Geert Greydanus" (Hoekstra 2013, p. 5)Geert form p. 1; Hoekstra 2013, pp. 4–7; Hendrik Satter, Oorlogsgravenstichting
1943–1944As "Oom Geert" he helps the Varsseveld group and the LO: placing people in hiding, arranging meetings, sheltering downed Allied pilots and distributing illegal newspapers (1952 Verzetsrapport and the letters of J. Satter and W.E. Braak-Raterink, written for the Stichting 1940–1945, which confirmed his resistance service on 13 Jan 1953). He also leads Sunday services for the people hiding on the farm (Hoekstra 2013, p. 7). Hoekstra, citing no source, writes the opposite about the resistance work: "Geert zal ook niet actief bij het verzetswerk betrokken zijn geweest. Dat was te riskant" (Geert will not have been actively involved in the resistance work either; that was too risky) (Hoekstra 2013, p. 5)Verzetsrapport; Satter letter; SEV report; Hoekstra 2013, p. 7
Dec 1943Geert panics and wants to give himself up. Hendrik Satter and Anne Schaap talk him out of it, and Wil Satter and her fiancé Schaap take him by train and bus to Schaap's parents' farm in Beetgum, Friesland, for a few weeks. A Friesland LO list signed by D.J. Autsema at Dantumawoude on 15 June 1945, of people he helped with hiding addresses, ration cards and identity papers, has the entry "Greijdanus, Gerrit, 38 j., Rotterdam West. Varkenoordseweg 359b.", hiding address "Beetgum. (Jelle v.d. Schaaf)", "ondergedoken rechercheur van politie R'dam. gehuwd". The address is the family home, so this is Geert, though the age is wrong (he was 44) and the first name is Gerrit, as in his cover name "Gerrit de Boer". Hoekstra names the Beetgum hosts as Schaap's parents; Autsema names Jelle van der Schaaf (NIOD 251a inv. 7, scan 0073)Hoekstra 2013, p. 6; Ben interview

20 Apr 1944The farm is surrounded. Geert, Hendrik Satter and Anne Schaap are arrested (Hoekstra says they were listening to the BBC). Geert had already gone to Friesland for his new "job" with the Trouw group, but "had to go back to Varsseveld once more to get his belongings" (Wietske Walinga, "The Greydanus History", p. 2). J. Satter's 1952 letter agrees: about a week after he left for Friesland he came to Varsseveld for a weekend, with Hendrik Satter and Schaap, and was arrested (J. Satter letter 1952). Ben says he had a premonition and threw his revolver and false IDs into the outhouse. The LO-LKP's Varsseveld report names the three: "On April 20, 1944, he struck. On that date, 3 persons were taken away here, namely Satter, Greydanus, and Schaap" (English rendering on archieven.nl) (NIOD 251a inv. 20, scan 138)Verzetsrapport; Hoekstra 2013, p. 8; Ben interview; Willy Markus (Wikipedia NL)
9 Aug 1944Leaves Vught on a transport to Kleve. Two helpers outside the camp write to Johanna that day. A handwritten letter, "Vught 9 Aug. 1944", signed "X", says that he left "naar Kleef in Duitsland" with 15 others, that they were on the platform at Vught at noon and "daar hebben wij hen rijkelijk voorzien van levensmiddelen" (we supplied them richly with food). A typed slip signed "N.N." says the writer spoke to him as he went on transport, "ik meen naar Kleef" (I think to Kleve), and that Geert asks her to stay strong "speciaal in verband met de vermeerdering in uw gezin" (especially in view of the increase in your family). The Buchenwald effects card later records his belongings coming "v. Haftanst. Kleve" (Arolsen 6004388). Hendrik Satter, who reached Kleve from Utrecht after 26 August, after Geert had left it, also says Geert had been there: "Greydanus also visited Kleef"Geert journal 13and14, 14b; Satter in Schaap account; NIOD 251a inv. 20, scan 140
14 Apr 1945A handwritten copy of the prison poem "Ik heb zooveel geleerd…", about faith in solitary confinement ("in „Einzelhaft" van alles schier beroofd"), is signed "Varsseveld 14 April 1945", two weeks after Varsseveld, near the Satter farm where Geert had hidden, was liberated. It is kept with Wietske Walinga's papers; the copyist is not named. The note under it credits the poem to Adolf Snijders, born 14 January 1920, written in prison in Utrecht and "gefusilleerd 26 Juli 1943". Snijders, of the resistance intelligence group Inlichtingen Dienst, was executed at the Leusderheide on 20 July 1943 according to the Oorlogsgravenstichting. The poem is printed in the Geuzenliedboek 1940–1945 as "Ter dood veroordeeld"Handwritten copy + transcription and translation; Oorlogsgravenstichting: Adolf Snijders; Geuzenliedboek no. 38

12 Aug 1945 (his own words)

The original two-page form, signed "G. Greydanus", Rotterdam, 12 Aug 1945, stamped by the Afwikkelingsbureau Concentratiekampen. Arrested "Varsseveld ten huize van H. SATTER op 20-4-1944", "Als ondergedoken politieman. (Had op 9-4-43 geweigerd, Joden te arresteeren", by "S.D. ARNHEM". Arrested with him: "Hendrik Satter ±60 j. landbouwer te Varsseveld en Anne Schaap, ±25 j. onderwijzer te Varsseveld". On the march: "Op Zondag (1e Paaschdag) 1945 werd het Lager opgebroken in verband m.h. oprukken der geall. Voetreis door Thüringen, zeer veel moeilijkheden. Op 10-4-1945 terug in Buchenwald en op 11-4-45 aldaar door de geall. bevrijd."

He lists fourteen fellow prisoners who had died and two who had been moved to other camps; the full list, with who each man was, is in The men Geert named.

And: "De fam. Rohaan – Heynen – v. Braak en Berlijn zijn door mij persoonlijk bezocht. [Ook heb] ik fam. van J. Stigter ontmoet." (I personally visited the families Rohaan, Heynen, v. Braak and Berlijn, and met the family of J. Stigter.) This is Geert's own confirmation of the Stigter visit recorded by the Kamp Vught museum

A Holland-America Line passage list for the Ryndam sailing of 5 Oct 1959 has "Greydanus G.", two fares, New York to Southampton, booked through Toronto (Stadsarchief Rotterdam 318-04 inv. 1164, scan 75).
