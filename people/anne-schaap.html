20 Apr 1944The farm is surrounded. Geert, Hendrik Satter and Anne Schaap are arrested (Hoekstra says they were listening to the BBC). Geert had already gone to Friesland for his new "job" with the Trouw group, but "had to go back to Varsseveld once more to get his belongings" (Wietske Walinga, "The Greydanus History", p. 2). J. Satter's 1952 letter agrees: about a week after Geert left for Friesland he came to Varsseveld for a weekend, with Hendrik Satter and Schaap, and was arrested (J. Satter letter 1952). Ben says his father had a premonition and threw his revolver and false IDs into the outhouse. The LO-LKP's Varsseveld report names the three: "On April 20, 1944, he [Markus] struck. On that date, 3 persons were taken away here, namely Satter, Greydanus, and Schaap" (English rendering on archieven.nl) (NIOD 251a inv. 20, scan 138)Verzetsrapport; Hoekstra 2013, p. 8; Ben interview; Willy Markus (Wikipedia NL)
20–21 Apr 1944The truck picks up Blaauwgeers and others in Lichtenvoorde, then goes to Avegoor (Ellecom), where prisoners are stripped and "Greydanus was in bad shape", and on to the SD building in Arnhem. On the way to Vught Jan Ket, Gerrit Kleisen and Henk Heinen jump from the truck (Schaap thinks one more did at Vught); Kleisen and Heinen are later brought into Vught bound. Freedom is not free names four men, all of whom got home: "Jan Ket, de Vries, Dick Vermey and Joop Thijssen" (p. 83). Three of them are confirmed elsewhere. Wikipedia NL has Jan Ket and Feitze de Vries jump "vanonder het dekzeil van de legerauto" near 's-Hertogenbosch. Dick Vermeij told the Arnhem special court in 1948 that he was taken from the SD prison in Arnhem "per auto naar Vught" and "Bij den Bosch sprong hij uit de wagen en kon de kuiten nemen" ("S.D.-man v. d. Berg", 5 Feb 1948). Thijssen is named only in the book; Kleisen and Heinen were sentenced to death on 2 June, so if they jumped they were caught again. It also has Jo Blaauwgeers stopped on his bicycle near Harreveld at dusk and taken away in a car, not picked up by the truck (p. 76)Schaap account, NIOD 251a pp. 7–8; KP-Aalten (Wikipedia NL); Freedom is not free, pp. 76, 83; "S.D.-man v. d. Berg", 5 Feb 1948
21 Apr – 9 Aug 1944Geert is held in Kamp Vught and tortured in the bunker; Hoekstra names the interrogators v.d. Berg, Enkelstrodt, Wemelink and "bunker executioner" Popey. Freedom is not free names F.H. Kohrmeijer and J.H. van de Berg, SD men in the 20 April arrests, as guards in the bunker, and describes "Popeye" creeping along the cells in sneakers (Freedom is not free, p. 85). Van de Berg was Joseph Heinrich van de Berg, an Arnhem police detective with a German mother who worked for the SD; through him "de Trouw-groep en de L.O.-beweging in de Achterhoek" suffered heavy losses, and witnesses described prisoners beaten with a club and a hose weighted with lead. Van de Berg is the "v.d. Berg" whom Hoekstra names among Geert's interrogators. The Arnhem special court sentenced van de Berg to death in February 1948. Kohrmeijer, a German SD man, appeared at that trial as a detainee; witnesses said he had been "correct" and had not beaten anyone himself ("S.D.-man v. d. Berg", 5 Feb 1948; report of the sentence, 14 Feb 1948). Ben describes beatings over a chair and alternating ice-cold and near-boiling baths. A fellow bunker prisoner, J. v.d. Hauw of Spannum, wrote on 16 Jun 1945 to a "Mevrouw Heinen" (probably the wife of Henk Heinen, named in Schaap's account) about her husband's arrival in the bunker "met de anderen uit Neede". V.d. Hauw says a Willem [surname unclear] "kwam vlak naast Greydanus te zitten in een cel recht tegenover mijn cel" (came to sit right next to Greydanus, in a cell directly opposite mine) (NIOD 251a inv. 325, scan 56)Verzetsrapport; Police letter 1952; Hoekstra 2013, p. 8; Ben interview
6 Jun 1944The Achterhoek group, including Jo Blaauwgeers, Gerrit Kleisen and Kees Poulie, is executed in the dunes at Overveen. Geert is classed as a political prisoner insteadSchaap account; Jo Blaauwgeers; Hoekstra 2013, p. 8
9 Aug 1944Geert leaves Vught on a transport to Kleve. Two helpers outside the camp write to Johanna that day. A handwritten letter, "Vught 9 Aug. 1944", signed "X", says that Geert left "naar Kleef in Duitsland" with 15 others, that they were on the platform at Vught at noon and "daar hebben wij hen rijkelijk voorzien van levensmiddelen" (we supplied them richly with food). A typed slip signed "N.N." says the writer spoke to Geert as he went on transport, "ik meen naar Kleef" (I think to Kleve), and that Geert asks her to stay strong "speciaal in verband met de vermeerdering in uw gezin" (especially in view of the increase in your family). The Buchenwald effects card later records his belongings coming "v. Haftanst. Kleve" (Arolsen 6004388). Hendrik Satter, who reached Kleve from Utrecht after 26 August, after Geert had left it, also says Geert had been there: "Greydanus also visited Kleef"Geert journal 13and14, 14b; Satter in Schaap account; NIOD 251a inv. 20, scan 140
12 Aug 1945 (his own words)

The original two-page form, signed "G. Greydanus", Rotterdam, 12 Aug 1945, stamped by the Afwikkelingsbureau Concentratiekampen. Arrested "Varsseveld ten huize van H. SATTER op 20-4-1944", "Als ondergedoken politieman. (Had op 9-4-43 geweigerd, Joden te arresteeren", by "S.D. ARNHEM". Arrested with him: "Hendrik Satter ±60 j. landbouwer te Varsseveld en Anne Schaap, ±25 j. onderwijzer te Varsseveld". On the march: "Op Zondag (1e Paaschdag) 1945 werd het Lager opgebroken in verband m.h. oprukken der geall. Voetreis door Thüringen, zeer veel moeilijkheden. Op 10-4-1945 terug in Buchenwald en op 11-4-45 aldaar door de geall. bevrijd."

Geert lists fourteen fellow prisoners who had died and two who had been moved to other camps; the full list, with who each man was, is in The men Geert named.

And: "De fam. Rohaan – Heynen – v. Braak en Berlijn zijn door mij persoonlijk bezocht. [Ook heb] ik fam. van J. Stigter ontmoet." (I personally visited the families Rohaan, Heynen, v. Braak and Berlijn, and met the family of J. Stigter.) This is Geert's own confirmation of the Stigter visit recorded by the Kamp Vught museum
