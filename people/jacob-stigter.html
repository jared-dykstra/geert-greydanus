Jacob (Jaap) Stigter (Honselersdijk, 1924 – Vught, 1944)

Jacob (Jaap) Stigter of Honselersdijk and KP-Neede, shot in Kamp Vught on 9 August 1944; Geert Greydanus told his family that Jaap had prayed for the prisoners in Vught.

Jacob Stigter, Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught

Jaap Stigter, 20, of KP-Neede, was shot in Kamp Vught on 9 August 1944, the day Geert left the camp. His brother-in-law R.M. Eising's memories, signed 9 December 2002, are the only outside account of what Geert said about Vught. They describe a Rotterdam policeman, spelled "Greidanus", who was arrested for refusing to arrest Jews, wrote to the Stigter family in the summer of 1945 and then visited them, and told them Jaap had been "tot grote steun" to the prisoners.

Geert's "Jaap Stigter, 19 j. uit het Westland". Born in Honselersdijk, in the Westland, on 23 July 1924, he worked in his father's horticultural business (tuinbouwbedrijf) and played the trombone in the village band. When the Germans called up young men for labour in Germany in 1943, he refused ("dat nooit!"), and on 23 June 1943 his brother-in-law took him by train to Zutphen, from where he went into hiding with a farmer in Neede; his parents were sheltering a Jewish couple at the time. In Neede he worked as a garden labourer and joined the KP. On a raid to seize weapons from a man thought to be working with the Germans, he was shot and wounded; the doctor who treated him was driving him away when both were arrested, and Jaap was taken to Kamp Vught. The museum says he may have been arrested on 20 April 1944, the day of the Achterhoek arrests, and that other sources give 24 May 1944. He was shot in Kamp Vught on 9 August 1944, the day Geert left the camp. Geert gave his age as 19; he turned 20 on 23 July (Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught: Stigter, Jacob).

His brother-in-law R.M. Eising wrote down the family's memories for the museum in 2002. After the liberation the family advertised in Trouw for news of Jaap, and a boy from Groningen told their minister, Ds. P. van Hoven, that he had been executed. That summer a letter came from "de heer Greidanus uit Rotterdam", a policeman arrested for refusing to arrest Jews, who had met Jaap in the prison at Vught. Geert then visited with his wife and told them that Jaap had been "tot grote steun" (a great support) to him and the other prisoners: "Als wij te bedroefd waren dan bad Jaap voor hen allen" (when we were too downcast, Jaap prayed for them all), and for "koningin en vaderland" (queen and country). "Dat Jaap in het geloof is gestorven, is voor zijn ouders, zijn meisje en voor ons allen tot een grote troost geweest" (that Jaap died in the faith has been a great comfort to his parents, his girlfriend and all of us) (Nationaal Monument Kamp Vught: Stigter, Jacob). Geert's own Red Cross form confirms the meeting: "[Ook heb] ik fam. van J. Stigter ontmoet" (see 12 Aug 1945).

12 Aug 1945 (his own words)

The original two-page form, signed "G. Greydanus", Rotterdam, 12 Aug 1945, stamped by the Afwikkelingsbureau Concentratiekampen. Arrested "Varsseveld ten huize van H. SATTER op 20-4-1944", "Als ondergedoken politieman. (Had op 9-4-43 geweigerd, Joden te arresteeren", by "S.D. ARNHEM". Arrested with him: "Hendrik Satter ±60 j. landbouwer te Varsseveld en Anne Schaap, ±25 j. onderwijzer te Varsseveld". On the march: "Op Zondag (1e Paaschdag) 1945 werd het Lager opgebroken in verband m.h. oprukken der geall. Voetreis door Thüringen, zeer veel moeilijkheden. Op 10-4-1945 terug in Buchenwald en op 11-4-45 aldaar door de geall. bevrijd."

Geert lists fourteen fellow prisoners who had died and two who had been moved to other camps; the full list, with who each man was, is in The men Geert named.

And: "De fam. Rohaan – Heynen – v. Braak en Berlijn zijn door mij persoonlijk bezocht. [Ook heb] ik fam. van J. Stigter ontmoet." (I personally visited the families Rohaan, Heynen, v. Braak and Berlijn, and met the family of J. Stigter.) This is Geert's own confirmation of the Stigter visit recorded by the Kamp Vught museum
