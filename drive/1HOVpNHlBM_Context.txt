/Geert journal/15
Jurgen Hoekstra notes the following:
...an interesting leaflet. It is an official investigation to 3 persons. Written on 3-5-1945 in Buchenwald.
1. Greydanus
2. Schepen
3. Palache
Schepen has been mentioned in the book of Niederorschel. He was an electrician. In Niederorschel he has for a long time been very sick. He came from Joure Friesland. So undoubtedly he must have spoken Frisian. So Geert and Ad Schepen must have spoken to each other. I tried to find him, but he did not live anymore. He had no children. After the war museum in Niederorschel has been erected, Schepen was a guest of honour.  


Palache is quite clear. Also a proof that Geert  and Leo P. must have been together. And also of course Ad Schepen.

/Geert journal - Part 2 (Page 30 to Page 37)
The 2nd section (Starting at #30) is separated with a gap and is in W. Walinga’s handwriting.  


Jurjen Hoekstra writes:
pages 30-37 are a complete description of the heavy bike-trip from Rotterdam to Frieland. They started with 4 girls, splitted up in the neighbourhood of Zwolle. Wietske with her friend went to Cubaard, the other two girls went to Drente. The way back was very difficult across the Afsluitdijk. Arriving in Amsterdam Wietske got an old paper and in that paper she read that her father was home again. It is nice to make the whole trip on paper. But that cost some time.


Jurgen Hoekstra has made an english translation as a separate document, including photos. - /Geert journal/30ff - VoedseltochtToFrieslandNoPicEnglish

/Papers In Wallet
A small wallet exists, which includes both of these papers. They must have had special significance, based on how they were stored.
Linda Walinga Writes:
Beppe Sakje did indeed write sermons. She filled a notebook with them, which I have. No one in the family wanted them. She did that before, during and after the war. It was the only intellectual activity she had. She only went to elementary school (not sure she was even allowed to finish it). She was a smart woman and wrote those sermons as an outlet.
Jared Dykstra Writes:
There were two papers folded and placed in a wallet, one typed and one handwritten.  I've no idea what the handwritten one is, but the typed one certainly appears to be a sermon.  Interestingly, as an addendum after the sermon (the typed document) there is a strongly worded appeal for secrecy.  It’s also interesting that this is noted as publication #7.
Linda Walinga Writes:
Yes! This appears to be a partial response. Beppe Sakje did not so much write sermons as commentaries on sermons, often relating to her own life and life circumstances. The printed sermon at a glance, begins with Paul of Tarsus as he initially goes out to spread the word of the Lord. 


Beppe Sakje's writing is hard to read, but she is commenting on the printed sermon she probably found it in a church publication, one of the few things she had to read.
