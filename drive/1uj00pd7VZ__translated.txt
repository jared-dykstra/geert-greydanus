I saw the car coming and had just listened to the radio. I had a printing press, fake P.B.'s, pedigrees, stamps, Parools, Trouw's, Je Maintiendrai's bicycle tires, etc. However, they found nothing. I was called by Bekker, who had another prisoner with him.


I got caught on the barbed wire, whereupon Bekker came to me and smashed my pipe in half, while some teeth also disappeared from my mouth. Then he asked me, "Where is Jo?" Then I thought: "That's what it's like to do Jo, so that's an opportunity for the detective and for me." (Greydanus had a false P.B. Rotterdam Municipal Police.


Satter was taken from the house and placed next to me.


I immediately noticed that they did not know my name.


The three of us had to stand next to each other with our hands up. One German stood behind the car, the others searched the house and found a box under the hay with a report of the robbery of the distribution office, registration cards, ration cards, insert sheets, P.B.'s, driver's license with a photo of me and a postcard by Wim Helming and Wim Hoffs.


At my interrogation I said that I knew nothing, and that I was only the fiancée of Satter's daughter. I kept this up. I had agreed this with Satter in advance.
Satter's radio came along. They also stole bacon, money boxes, etc. They were asked where Satter's daughter was, but she was not to be seen.
Then we drove away. The car stopped in Lichtenvoorde and Blauwgeers, Kettering, Wortman and also Wim Helming's brother were loaded there. They looked for Wim Helming on the sports field, but he had fled.
When we arrived in Avegoor, we all had to undress and were only allowed to keep our shirts on. We then had to stand in the corridors and later again like pegs in a barrel in the wagon, in layers on top of each other. Greydanus was in bad shape, but the Krauts walked over us with their heavy hobnail boots. Then we went to Arnhem, to the S.D. building. on the Utrechtschestraat. We stood there in the hallway all night without clothes. I ended up next to Wiggers from the Woldboom. This man told me that he had done nothing and that he had a heart problem. In the hallway I met Henk Heinen from Eibergen.
Later we were loaded back into the same car.


I was sitting in the car, fiddling with the lock, when someone asked: Is there one that has a knife or something like that? A kind of nail file was then produced and we used it to loosen the sail from the side plank. When they stopped in front of a bridge, Jan Ket jumped out of the car. After that, two more jumped out (Gerrit Kleisen and Henk Heinen), another one in Vught, I think. The Krauts were furious and tried everything to catch those who had fled. Later, in Vught, we saw Gerrit Keisen and Henk Heinen brought in bound. We were all put in the bunker.
