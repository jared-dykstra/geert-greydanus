Preface


The hunger journey of Wietske Greydanus


The enclosed story is based on the diary of Wietske Greydanus. She was 21 years old when she started a journey on bicycle to Friesland, to try to get food, because people in Rotterdam were starving.
She started the journey together with 3 friends, on an old-fashioned bicycle without gears and with very old tires. These old tires would cause a lot of problems later on. Big parts of the journey had to be walked rather than biked. In addition finding enough food to survive the journey, and finding decent places to stay and sleep was very difficult.
What is very striking is that the trip started on March 21, when the war was still ongoing. And ended on May 22, when the war was well over.


A very adventurous trip indeed!
  























                                                 


Wietske Greydanus * 04-03-1924  †28-07-2008




Jurjen Hoekstra- februari 2021[a]




























     The map of Holland which the route Wietske had made   


2.
Friesland March 21- May 22, 1945


The four of us went out to find food. Toos and Annerie Numan planned to go to Ruinerwold. Truus Maaskant and I planned to go to Friesland.
The weather was beautiful when we started. We kept wondering however whether we were doing the right thing. And what we were going to do if the Tommies arrived before we were back.
The first day we wanted to reach Harderwijk (ed: well over a 100 km), and we succeeded. From Harderwijk we planned to sail with a skipper in the direction of Friesland and Drenthe.
  































            The small harbour of Harderwijk




Unfortunately we were too late and the skipper had just left, so that this opportunity was
lost. We walked and searched for two hours to find accommodations for the night. Finally we found a place to sleep with Schuitemaker, where Truus and I slept in a small bed.
In the morning we started of without breakfast, so we queued at Fino for soup from a soup kitchen. Afterwards we biked to Jet but she was not at home. We were really at a loss what to do, now that the skipper had left.
Truus and I decide to cycle to Sonnevanck (ed: a sanatorium close to Harderwijk), where we visited Anne Huyzer and where we could eat. In the afternoon we saw Toos and Annerie again. We decided to cycle to Heerde where Ina Lock lived, to ask if she knew a way to get over the river IJssel. On the road we quit often heard the sound of guns.
We could all sleep at Mrs. de Jonge. The next day we biked to Wapenveld as we had heard rumours that it might be possible to cross the river IJssel from there. In Wapenveld we saw Mrs. Rutgers, but she couldn't help us. We went to the bank of the river IJssel, but we did not see any possibility for crossing it.
We decided that the best option for us was to try to get some rye and than go back to Rotterdam, go home.
In the afternoon I tried one last time to find a way across the IJssel. And I managed to get across! In a leaky boat with it’s oars missing.
3.


  



































    The map on which the place where Wietske and her girlfriends were crossing the river IJssel
    between Wapenveld and Windesheim.


We got to the other side with great difficulty. While laboriously crossing the river, we drifted down at least 2 river widths and landed close to Windesheim. Fortunately there were no checks or patrols. In the evening we heard a lot of shooting, we slept in the hay of farmer Kamphuis. The next day we biked to Oudeschoot. The old tires were punctured often, we had to walk a lot, and we heard guns a lot. If only we had stayed at home!


At Meppel, Truus and Annerie went in a different direction.
We walked from Steenwijk to Wolvega, where we got a lift by horse and carriage, followed by another long walk. At 9 o'clock in the evening we arrived in O. where a very warm welcome awaited us.
  



























                     The small village of Cubaard
4.
We stayed there on Sunday and on Monday left for Cubaard. With two new tires from Uncle Jan on our bikes. In Irnsum we got food at a public soup kitchen. A visit to a farmer in the neighbourhood was successful, we each got a pound of butter. We arrived in Cubaard at 5 o'clock in the afternoon, where we were also received very warmly.
The next day we went to Anjum. On our way we went to the dairy factory in Dronrijp and visited Pe Woudsma in Birdaard.
At 7 o'clock in the evening we arrived in Anjum. The first thing Gerard and Cor asked was: “How is Bensje?”. (ed: This must have been Ben Greydanus, who was 1 year old at the time)
We stayed in Anjum for the Eastern-weekend. It was simply too risky to leave for Rotterdam, so we had to wait and see. We visited Bouma very often to get the latest news: the Tommies were advancing quickly. On Tuesday we went to Genum. Gerard picked me up again on Saturday, because Aunt Klas had fallen ill. Friesland was liberated on April 13. The NBS occupied the Schans.
On Sunday, Germans from Schiermonnikoog tried to land near Oostmahorn, which failed. On Monday they shelled Oostmahorn and tried to land again. 2 Canadians were killed during the fight.
We stayed in Anjum for about a week. Then left for Genum in the hope that soon the whole country would be liberated and we could go home again. Truus stayed the whole time at the Kooiplaats in Anjum. The old rear tire of my bike disintegrated on the journey to Genum, and repair was impossible. We asked ourselves how we would ever get home again.
The plan we came up with was that as soon as the country was liberated, Uncle Klaas would take me home on his motorbike. I wrote that to Truus, and she then left to go home through Drenthe.
With our newly gained freedom came a prohibition to travel. I tried everywhere to get a travel-permit, without success. I went back to Anjum, to see if Fokke Rosier could help me, see if he knew of a transport to Rotterdam. On Wednesday in Anjum I participated in the festive parade of decorated wagons. I dressed up as Musssert's aunt (May 1940, Mussert Geert in the haystack).
On Thursday Jan came and told me that I could hitch a ride with 2 American soldiers to Haarlem. I was warned however that it was unlikely that I would be allowed to cross the Afsluitdijk. I decide to try anyway. We left at 5 o'clock on a Saturday morning.


  



















A kind of residence permit that Wietske had for her stay in Oostdongeradeel.
So she was a hunger refugee.


5.
Miraculously I was allowed to cross the Afsluitdijk and everything went well up to Den Oever. In the Wieringermeer there was still some resistance of German soldiers. I was not safe to continue and I had to stay with the NBS in (Sint ?) Hypppolytushoef.


Later I was brought to Melle Montsma and could stay there. On Whit-Monday it was possible to travel again and I left early. I had a new solid rear tire on my bike and a heavy load on the back (± 40 kg). The bike was almost impossible to move forward. And it was pouring with rain all day.
In Purmerend I stayed with the Verloop family. I happened to get hold of an old issue from Trouw, and in it I read that my father was still alive!
In the evening I went to Amsterdam where there was everything was under strict control. Fortunately I was allowed to keep everything. In Amsterdam I stayed with the van der Hilst family. In the evening we had to repack, as everything was still soaking wet and the bags were torn.
The next morning I left for home, finally! During the whole way to Rotterdam there was a strong headwind. But underway I managed to get a bag of pears. I arrived home at 8 o’clock in the evening. My father had been home for more than a week by then!
